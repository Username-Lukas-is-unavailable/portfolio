2) Dead Man's Switch

Back in 2022, I wanted to use a dead man’s switch for a project I was working on, but I ended up coming to the conclusion that the specific type of way I needed the dead man’s switch to be didn’t seem to exist on any website or program yet. So I started making my own.


Idea
A dead man’s switch is a device that requires a regular input to stay in either an activated or deactivated state. Like a train driver having to press a button every so often to make sure they’re still awake and conscious or a grenade without the pin, which will start detonation as soon as the person holding it lets go of the lever. I wanted a button of the former type, with a customisable timer length. The user would have to press a button to keep the timer above from reaching zero. If the timer runs out the player loses.


Old execution of the interactivity
Back then, for my use case, this website (which can be found on my old replit ), really only needed to work once, because I would have lost the game after the timer ran out, so when halfway through development the reset function stopped working, it wasn’t a priority to fix. It still does not work to this day. The front-end is very plain and rather unimpressive. The button is essentially the only interactive thing on there. The social media logos do a little css animation when they are hovered over. The colours of the background change as the time goes down, to indicate the growing urgency of pushing the button. Sound effects also indicate the state of the game.

My old Dead Man's Switch website (2022)


What I would do differently now
There’s definitely a few things I’d do differently. First of all I’d want to use my better understanding of JS to improve on a few things, though functionally it would remain largely the same. I’d want to add a few additional UI/UX features such as a timer tracking how long you’ve been alive for, as a way of keeping score, as well as a functioning reset button (currently the only way to reset is to reload the page).

Additionally, my coach gave me feedback, and suggested adding more interaction, more moving elements. Such as a progress bar or animations to improve on the feeling of gamification. He also suggested I improve the visuals and UI.


Reimagined Version
For my portfolio I made an updated version that you can find here, or on the projects page!

My new updated Dead Man's Switch website

Play Dead Man's Switch

The design of the reimagined version is based off of my first version. The old version functioned as my prototype design. The original design was simple and minimalist. It didn't need to be much more and I didn't want it to distract. It's largely very faithful, though I tweaked some of the colours to make it consistent with the main page. I linked back to the main website using a button, for easier, more intuitive navigation back to the main site.

I also updated the css so the site scales better. Additionally, I added a progress bar that shows visually how much time is left. Using some simple math it calculates the remaining percentage of time. This adds another layer of feedback into the UI. Now the colour of the background, the timer element and the progress bar all help the user to understand how much time is left.

Next, to enhance the feeling of gamification, I added a total score. This score tracks the total time the player has stayed alive. It automatically stops when you lose, using the same logic that changes the backgrounds dynamically based on the state of the game, which was present in the earlier version. I used the knowledge I gained from making the music player on the Homepage. After a game over the High-Score gets updated, and using Bluesky's documentation I was able to add a share button, that will generate a pre-populated post with the player's High-Score. It also pings me, so I get to see your awesome High-Scores!
I feel these additions make it feel a lot more like a little game. You can share your high scores and compare them with your friends' scores.

Lastly I want to add a character that reflects the state of the game. If you lose she'd die. I have not yet implemented this.

Ultimately I feel I've made the feel of the application a lot more appealing. I have also added more feedback to the UI.

The pre-populated BlueSky post includes your high-score


Reflection
I'm happy with the updates I have made. They're not super large in scope, but I feel they added a lot to the experience. I had always wondered how to make pre-populated posts, so learning how to make those was fun! (and a lot simpler than I had thought)
I wish I had had time to add the character, that reflects the state of the game. It would've been a cute addition, that would have given the page a lot more personality. I also would have loved to have added the information box. These are things I'd love to add in the future!

I think it's super cool how this project ties in with my portfolio site. Making the old version was the first time I used JS to play sounds, and one of the first times I used JS in general. The knowledge from then was a very helpful foundation for making the sounds for my portfolio site work. The knowledge I got from making the portfolio sounds (and the rest of my portfolio site) were in turn super helpful making this updated version!!

This site might look familiar



I have made a portfolio website!! (You're looking at it).
It features a bunch of interactive elements with visual and audio feedback. I played around with animations, sound effects and more.

The rough design concept I made in semester 1



Presentation, Theme and Vibe
For this website I really tried to channel the feeling of old personal websites, such as MySpace pages or old 90s/00s personal websites, while making it slightly more modern and user-friendly. I also tried to channel some of the philosophy behind DVD menus, specifically of children's media. I tried to have my hobby, drawing and art, really shine through by hand-drawing a bunch of elements.

I did all of this because I tried to keep the site a little playful, with a healthy dose of whimsy, while also keeping it inviting and trying not to compromise on usability. Some professional portfolio sites can tend to be modern, sober, sterile and in my personal opinion, very boring. I don't think the more conventional style of portfolio websites fits me as a person. I try to keep a healthy dose of childlike whimsy and silliness in my life, because adult life is usually boring enough. I'm not afraid to let my inner child out sometimes.
I spent a decade of my life struggling with my chronic depression, impostor syndrome and anxiety, all of which I still experience. Humour and silliness has always been one of my main ways of coping. If life insists on being boring so often, I'll take initiative and try to keep things fun for myself. And for others too. I love whenever I'm able to make people smile. I try to make the small parts of this so often cruel and wretched world that I interact with a little better. So it makes me really happy my silly site with silly sound effects has made some people smile, if even just a little.

I talk more about this in the section about my Core Values (5.1).

I wish I could have added even more little hidden details, but I did not have the time.
Fun details I included are things like hovering over the page title, sound effects and little playfully interactive elements in the "Hotbar" on the Homepage.


Colour Choice
The main colour palette of the site is shades of pink and purple. These are my favourite colours. I tried to make the text readable, making sure the colour is different enough. The different main pages have a unique banner colour to help distinguish them. I added css animations to almost all clickable elements, to provide very obvious feedback to the user as to what's clickable (and also just because I thought they were neat)



The primary colour palette and text colour contrast tests



Sound Effects
I tried to keep the site a little playful. One way I attempted to do this was by adding sound effects to elements upon clicking and sometimes hovering. I used my JS knowledge from our development lectures and workshops at school in combination with my previous experience adding sounds to web projects to do this.

I really like the sound effects, they make me think of DVD menus, from which my website pulls inspiration. The sound effects on the custom image-based buttons brings me back to 2000s web design. I love websites from that era with sound effects on buttons and such. I also feel they add to the UX, ever so slightly, by giving some audio feedback. It also adds more of a feeling of interactivity. It adds another sense, being hearing, to the experience.

I also just thought they would be a fun feature to implement. They were.
Multiple people, including teachers, indicated they liked the addition of sound effects. The feature was praised by some for its uniqueness.


Music Player
I made a music player that plays my own custom-made songs, which can be found on the homepage. When the user clicks the gramophone in the Hotbar, the playlist starts playing. Once a song is actively playing the gramophone gets little icon added onto it, indicating the next click will skip the song. When a song finishes on its own the icon disappears and the user can start the music again by clicking on the gramophone once more. The icons I used are established icons popularised on TV remotes, skip and play.


Implementation of UX Feedback
I showed the site to classmates in real life, friends from secondary school over discord and my psychologist while on a Zoom call. People liked the addition of this music player, though a much requested feature was a way to... you know... stop the music. I did not initially have this functionality. At that time the only way to make it stop was reloading the page. This wasn't particularly user-friendly. In version 1.5.4 - Music Player User Feedback I added a stop button. Initially I wanted it to only appear when a song was playing, but decided to have it always visible so users can notice it before playing the song, so they can quickly turn it off again, without having to find the stop button first. I also made the stop button highlighted once a song starts playing, to draw attention to it. The sound the button makes depends on whether it's in the functional state or not.


Known Flaws in the UI
The navbar links are images, but they were the first images functioning as UI buttons I added. Back then I did this by having an image with a transparent hole get a background through css upon hover. This worked fine until I wanted to make them move upon hover as well. Now the background sometimes shows a little when it's moving. I wanted to update these to work like the pagedoll or logo in the top corner, using css to swap out the background image, but wasn't able to due to time constraints.


Reflection
I am very proud of my cute website. I love the way it turned out. I think the design is super fun, and I loved making it interactive! I want to do more!! Making my portfolio website was the most fun web project I've worked on in a long time! I want to make it into my new personal website on my own custom domain one day!

3.3) Tools and Software


When drawing or designing digitally I almost always use Paint.NET, which is an image and photo editing software for computers running windows’ .NET framwork. It is praised for its intuitive and innovative user interface and relative ease of use.

The program started out as a free, more advanced replacement for MS Paint, developed as an undergraduate college senior design project mentored by Microsoft, but the project has grown significantly in scope over time and has left the shadow of MS Paint. Paint.NET is still updated regularly, but the software also has a sizable community who have created a lot of plugins and user created expansions to the core program, which are readily available online.
The software is pretty powerful, yet elegantly simple. It has been compared to other digital photo editing software packages such as Adobe Photoshop, Corel Paint Shop Pro and The GIMP.
I personally use the version obtained from the official Paint.NET website , which despite its, eh… simplistic web design is easy to navigate. There is also a version available on the Microsoft Store, but I just simply prefer to have the independent version.


Plugins
I personally use the plugins included in BoltBait’s BoltBaitPack plugin pack . These add a bunch of functionality and more advanced versions of existing features, but they’re honestly not needed. I only started using these plugins during my study, before then I had always just used vanilla Paint.NET. The only feature I’d love to see in the program that isn’t included, nor have I found plugins for yet, would be a liquify tool. The base features of the vanilla program are already advanced, with the application for example having Bokeh Blur built-in, something even some bigger name brand programs do not feature by default.


Drawing Hardware
For drawing I use my laptop. Up until last year I drew using a Wacom Intuos3 6x8 drawing tablet from 2004. This old thing is as big as my laptop, but it’s still regarded by many as the best tablet wacom ever made, including some wacom employees. Unfortunately it is no longer supported and drivers are no longer updated. While I no longer use it to draw with, I still use it for video editing, a hobby of mine.

A Wacom Intuos3 6x8


In 2024, I upgraded to my current drawing tablet, a Wacom One with Pen Display. I had been wanting to get a drawing tablet with a screen for a while, but they had always been a little too pricey in the past. Last year I was finally able to buy a good one for a good deal, with the price of these tablets having gone down over the years, in addition to a reduced price. I am absolutely in love with this thing and have been drawing more digital art than ever before. It’s also very nice for design work. The one downside is that it doesn’t exactly encourage a great posture.

3.4) Iterative Design


Examples of iterative design in my projects.

Dead Man's Switch

After I had updated my Dead Man's Switch project, I let users test the webpage. One tester noted that the text "Press the button when the time is low" on the button's green state, could be misinterpreted. They feared that without context, this could be interpreted as meaning you have to press the button as close to the timer hitting zero as possible. This is not the intention.
4.1 Research - Unclear Text on the Button During the Green phase

The old text could potentially be misinterpreted

In version 1.5.11 of my portfolio site I updated the Dead Man's Switch page in accordance with the user feedback. I changed the problematic text with the text "Press the button before the timer runs out".
Version 1.5.11 - "1.5.11 - Portfolio Update - More ReadMe Files and small DMS update" on my Github

The new text is hopefully clearer

I approached different test subjects to test this new 1.5.11 version of the page, and asked them to describe what they think the intended gameplay was based off of only the button's green state. Most people got it right, one didn't. I then showed the next state of the button (orange) and asked again, they all now understood the basic gameplay.

I also asked the user who had alerted me of the potential issue. They agreed that this new text was a lot clearer.
More research results and feedback can be found in the LO 4 section.


Portfolio Site

I programmed a music player on my website. After letting users test the music player, the main point of critique and feedback was that they were missing a stop button, to turn off the music.
4.1 Research - The Music Player Could Really use a Stop Button

The old player did not have a way to stop the music

In version 1.5.4 of my portfolio site I updated the music player in accordance with the user feedback. I added a button to turn off the music. The button was hidden when no music was playing, and would show up when the music started.

After letting a user test this new version of the music player, before pushing the update, I got the feedback that the stop button was hard to find, because they couldn't spot it before the music started. The user did not notice the button appearing, and was not able to turn off the music easily.

I removed the part of the code that hid the button and replaced it with some code that instead made it so the audio feedback would be different, depending on whether music was playing and the button worked, or whether the music was not playing, meaning the button wouldn't do anything. I also added a highlight to the button when music starts, in the hope it catches the user's attention once they turn on the music.
Version 1.5.4 - "Portfolio Update - Music Player User Feedback" on my Github

A stop button now exists and is highlighted while music is playing

I released 1.5.4 and asked people to test it. The results indicated that the stop button was appreciated and was usable. People were able to turn off the music.

During the testing of this feature, a bug was found with the stop button's highlight in certain non-chromium browsers. This issue is still present. I will probably try to fix this in a future update.

More research results and feedback can be found in the LO 4 section.

5.1) Core Values

There’s a few principles and beliefs that I find important, not just in work, but in life as a whole.

Creativity & Curiosity
People have always described me as a creative person, and I’ve always enjoyed using my creativity. In life, I have found incredible solace in making creative works in all kinds of media. It can work wonderfully as an escape from "real" life. I’m eager to learn and broaden my skill set. When it comes to creative work, I'm most always down to at the least try out new things, such as media or presentation.


Authenticity and the Human Element
Being true to myself is important to me. I do not care much for society’s expectations, I live by my core values, that’s what’s most important to me. I am unapologetically myself. I try to keep a healthy dose of childlike whimsy and silliness in my life, because adult life is usually boring enough. I'm not afraid to let my inner child out sometimes.

I spent a decade of my life struggling with my chronic depression, impostor syndrome and anxiety, all of which I still experience. Humour and silliness has always been one of my main ways of coping. If life insists on being boring so often, I'll take initiative and try to keep things fun for myself. And for others too. I love whenever I'm able to make people smile. I try to make the small parts of this so often cruel and wretched world that I interact with, just a little better how I found them.

I also very much value the human element of things. I am very much not a fan of generative AI, especially not when it’s used to outsource creativity. As an artist and designer, I will personally avoid using generative AI as much as possible.

I do not agree with the ethical issues the tech brings with it. Additionally, I would just never want to have a machine take over and automate the work I love doing. The desire to automate boring and repetitive stuff, I can understand, but I’d never want to give up something I am passionate about only for some algorithm to pump out some soulless, generic thing that is merely an average of its dataset, often built upon stolen works. The slop that generative AI produces is devoid of soul and I simply want nothing to do with it.

I believe that the tech has its applications, for example, it can be used in medical science to help recognise diseases more quickly and stuff like that is truly wonderful, but the tech is not perfect yet, but is nonetheless rushed and being hastily added to anything and everything, requires insane computing power and is incredibly damaging to the climate.

I am of the belief that if you are going to use such damaging tech, it should at be for such use cases as saving lives in the medical world, but I am utterly disgusted that the climate, and with it my future, are being sacrificed to “replace” creative individuals who were already struggling to get by, only for their replacement to be soulless slop, an average of data, devoid of meaning and any humanity, trained off of their work without their consent.

It is truly depressing.

I believe the whole GenAI hype is a bubble, much like the dot-com bubble. Every company is forcefully shoving it down users' throats, lowering privacy standards even more as it trains off of all the data, including personal data, there is to find. But in their money-hungry race to include as much GenAI and/or related buzzwords in their products, companies are very much alienating large portions of the population. Whenever I see GenAI related buzzwords in a company's description, advertisement or elsewhere, it tells me very clearly in an instant that they're not worth my time, and not worth interacting with. I usually block them on sight.

The current state of AI online genuinely wears on my already poor mental health. I find the craze overhyped and the way the tech is used en masse appalling and disgusting.

I will always support human artists and human-made content over the sickening soullessly generated slop GenAI shits out.

Niek, Myself and Malou at The Art Department 2025
